Start here: a quick self-check

As at 2 November 2026 the official list of high risk supports hasn't been published. These five questions show you which parts of the changes are worth a closer look. If you're not sure, treat it as a yes.

  1. Are you a sole trader or small provider delivering supports funded under participants' plans? From 1 December 2026, claims will need to be made within 90 days of the support being delivered, by whoever claims (you, the plan manager or the participant). And as a provider, you'll need to keep records about those supports for 7 years from the day each claim is made, whoever makes it.
  2. Do you deliver personal care or daily living supports? These are among the examples the Government has given for the expansion that starts rolling out from 1 July 2027.
  3. Do you deliver supports in closed settings or supported independent living (SIL), support participants whose funding is NDIA-managed, or run an NDIS digital platform? The NDIS Commission describes a digital platform as an online app, website or system that acts as an intermediary between participants and the people providing their supports, where payments are processed through it using NDIS amounts from participants' plans, and whose main purpose is connecting participants with supports from their plan. Mandatory registration for SIL and digital platforms started on 1 July 2026, with transitional arrangements.
  4. Are you unsure whether your supports are in scope? That's normal while the official list is still to come.
  5. Could you find the records for every support you've been paid for since 27 August 2026? NDIS providers have to keep records about their claims and the supports they delivered for 7 years from the day each claim is made. This applies to claims made from 27 August 2026, including when a plan manager or the participant makes the claim. The NDIS rules will set out which records.

What's already changed in 2026

  • The new law was passed by Parliament on 19 August 2026 and got Royal Assent on 20 August 2026.
  • NDIS providers have to keep records about their claims and the supports they delivered for 7 years from the day each claim is made. This applies to claims made from 27 August 2026, including when a plan manager or the participant makes the claim.
  • From 1 December 2026, claims will need to be made within 90 days of the support being delivered, by whoever claims (you, the plan manager or the participant). It used to be 2 years.
  • Since 15 October 2026 the NDIA has been doing extra checks on claims made more than 90 days after the support, and can hold those payments for up to 28 days.
  • Mandatory registration for SIL and NDIS digital platforms started on 1 July 2026, with transitional arrangements.
  • From 1 July 2027 most providers will need to enrol with the NDIA. That's separate from registration.

High risk supports: what's known as at 2 November 2026

The Government says it will publish a list of high risk supports. The examples it's given are personal care, daily living supports and supports in closed settings. Expanded registration starts rolling out from 1 July 2027, and everyone in scope has to be registered by December 2030. There's no date yet for the official list.

What registration might cost

ABC News reported industry estimates of anywhere from a few thousand dollars to $15,000 or more, much of it from mandatory third-party audits. The NDIS Minister said audit costs would be set by the market. That's why the workbook will include a planner for comparing quotes from approved quality auditors before you commit.

Questions

Do I need to be a registered NDIS provider?

It depends on what you deliver and how your participants' funding is managed. Some supports already need registration, such as supports to participants with NDIA-managed funding. Mandatory registration for SIL and digital platforms started on 1 July 2026, with transitional arrangements. From 1 July 2027 registration starts to expand to high risk supports, with everyone in scope registered by December 2030. The official list isn't out yet.

Should I wait until the official list is published?

You can. Some things don't depend on the list, though. The 90-day claiming rule starts on 1 December 2026, and providers already have to keep records for claims made since 27 August 2026 for 7 years from the claim date, whoever makes the claim. The free Starter Pack covers those now.

Will this tell me whether I must register?

No. It helps you work out which questions apply to you. Its results are “Rules may already apply today: check with the Commission”, “likely affected” or “to verify”. Only the official rules, and the NDIS Commission, can settle it.

What is the NDIS 90 day rule?

From 1 December 2026, claims will need to be made within 90 days of the support being delivered, by whoever claims (you, the plan manager or the participant). The 90 days run from the day of the support, not the day you invoice. Before that the limit was 2 years.
